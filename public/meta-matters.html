The move

After a quiz, students answer three prompts: How did I prepare? What worked? What will I change?

  1. Return the quiz1 minuteLet students look at their score.
  2. Prompt 11 minute“How did you prepare? Be honest: what exactly did you do, and for how long?”
  3. Prompt 21 minute“Look at what you got right and wrong. What worked?”
  4. Prompt 31 minute“What will you do differently next time? Pick one strategy.”
  5. Model one strategy2 minutesPost a short list of proven options: quiz yourself, space it out, explain it to someone, mix up problem types. Then show one with this unit’s content, for example turning notes into three questions and answering them from memory.

Why it works

Many students study the same way whether or not it works. Thinking about how you learn, called metacognition, is one of the highest-impact, lowest-cost strategies in education research. Linking study habits to actual results helps students choose better strategies next time. Reflection alone isn’t enough, though: the research supports teaching the strategies explicitly, which is why you model one before students leave.

Watch out for

  • “I’ll study harder”: push for a specific strategy.
  • Only doing it after bad grades: do it every time.
  • Rereading as the plan: it’s the least effective option; steer toward self-quizzing.

Level up

  • Students test a new strategy before the next quiz and report back.
  • Compare two quizzes’ reflections: did the change work?

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: give a checklist of strategies to circle.
  • IEP and 504 supports: a short conference instead of writing.
  • Early finishers: teach a classmate their best strategy.

Tech option

A three-question form that saves answers over the year.
