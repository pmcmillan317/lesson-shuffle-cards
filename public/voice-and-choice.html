The move

Each week, hand students one real decision, like co-writing a rubric line or picking a due date from two options.

Before class: Pick the decision

  1. Pick the decisionBefore classSomething real, with limits you’re fine with either way.
  2. Explain the limits30 seconds“This week you decide: the project is due Thursday or Friday. Either works for me.”
  3. Discuss2–3 minutes“Give me one reason for each option.”
  4. Decide1 minuteVote by hands or a quick private poll.
  5. Honor it30 seconds“Friday it is. Your call.”Then stick to it.

Why it works

When every decision comes from the teacher, rules feel imposed. A meta-analysis of studies on choice found that giving people choices increased motivation, effort and performance. Small, bounded choices work best.

Watch out for

  • Fake choices: only offer options you’ll truly accept.
  • Choices with no limits: two or three options keep it manageable.
  • The loudest voices deciding: use a private vote.

Level up

  • Students propose the decision they want to make next.
  • A rotating student team runs the vote.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: show options with pictures or icons.
  • IEP and 504 supports: make sure every option still meets required accommodations.
  • Students who disagree with the result: offer a private alternative when possible.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyShared Google Doc in suggesting modeStudents propose wording for a rubric line as suggestions; the class accepts the final version together.
  • AugmentMentimeter ranking or Google FormsA private vote shows the real preference, not the loudest one.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Give me 10 real decisions I can hand to a [grade] class each week, each with two options I am fine with either way, from due dates to rubric wording to the order of topics. Before you finish: Only include decisions I can actually honor. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
