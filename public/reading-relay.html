Reading Relay

Read it, but didn’t get it?

15–20 minOne-time setupStrong

The move

Groups of four each take a role card: Summarizer, Questioner, Clarifier, Predictor. Everyone reads one section silently, then each role takes a turn out loud. Pass the cards left for the next section.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 3Hand out cards1 minute
  2. 4Read a section2 minutes
  3. 5Roles take turns2 minutes
  4. 6Pass left10 seconds
  5. 7Close2 minutes

Before class (step 1): Split the reading

  1. Split the readingBefore class (2 minutes)Mark the reading in 4 sections of about 2 minutes of silent reading each. Print the role cards once and keep each set of four in an envelope.
  2. Model onceFirst time only, in class: 5 minutesRead a paragraph aloud and do all four roles yourself, naming each one. Use the example lines below.
  3. Hand out role cards1 minuteEach group of four gets one set. Each student takes one role card.“Read the card you got. That’s your job for the first section.”
  4. Read one section silently2 minutesEveryone reads the same section silently.
  5. Each role takes a turn out loud2 minutesIn order: Summarizer, Questioner, Clarifier, Predictor. Each says their part using the card. Example lines are below.
  6. Pass the cards left10 secondsThen read the next section. Repeat for all four sections, so everyone plays every role.“Pass your card to the left.”
  7. Close2 minutesEach group shares its best question with the class.

What each role says

Use these lines to model the roles. The printed role cards have them too.

Summarizer

Says the most important idea in one or two sentences.

  • “This part was mostly about how the water cycle moves water.”
  • “The main idea is that the author wants us to…”

Questioner

Asks one question the text answers and one it makes them wonder.

  • “Who can find where it says why the colonies protested?”
  • “I wonder why the author left out…”

Clarifier

Picks a tricky word or idea and helps the group figure it out.

  • “A tricky word was evaporate. I think it means to turn into gas because the text says…”
  • “I’m confused by this sentence. Can we read it again?”

Predictor

Uses clues from the text to guess what comes next.

  • “I predict the next section is about rain, because the text says the water cools.”
  • “I think the character will… because…”

Why it works

Strong readers do these four things as they read. Giving each of these a role makes the invisible habits visible, and rotating roles means every student practices all four. This approach, called reciprocal teaching, has been studied since the 1980s (Palincsar & Brown, 1984).

Watch out for

  • Summaries that retell everything: limit it to two sentences.
  • Questions with one-word answers: ask the Questioner for one “why” or “how” question.
  • Groups racing ahead: signal each new section as a whole class at first.
  • Sections too long: if silent reading runs past 3 minutes, use shorter sections or fewer of them.

Level up

  • No cards: each group covers all four roles on its own.
  • Use a harder text once roles feel automatic.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: the sentence starters on each card do the heavy lifting; add home-language versions if you can.
  • IEP and 504 supports: offer an audio version of the text or let a partner read aloud.
  • Early finishers: write a fifth role, such as Connector.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyGoogle Docs commentsEach role leaves comments on the shared text that start with its name (“Questioner:”). You can read every group’s thinking afterward, not just hear one.
  • ModifyDiffit or Brisk Teaching (free tiers)Make leveled versions of the same text in minutes so every group reads the same content at a reachable level.
  • AugmentChrome Reading modeRead aloud and adjustable text support students who decode slowly, without a separate version.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Here is a text: [paste text]. Split it into 4 sections at natural breaks. For each section write: a model summary in 2 sentences, 2 good questions, the 2 words or phrases most likely to confuse my students with plain explanations, and a reasonable prediction. This is my answer key for the four roles. Before you finish: Keep summaries to 2 sentences each. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.

Evidence

Strong

Palincsar, A. S., & Brown, A. L. (1984). Reciprocal teaching of comprehension-fostering and comprehension-monitoring activities. Cognition and Instruction, 1(2), 117–175. The original study of these four roles.

Rosenshine, B., & Meister, C. (1994). Reciprocal teaching: A review of the research. Review of Educational Research, 64(4), 479–530. Across many studies, reciprocal teaching improved reading comprehension.
