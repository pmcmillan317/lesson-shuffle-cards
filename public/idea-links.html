Idea Links

Snap the ideas together

The move

Groups write one idea per sticky note, then arrange the notes so every pair that touches is a connection they can explain.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 1Generate3 minutes
  2. 2Link5 minutes
  3. 3Label2 minutes
  4. 4Defend3 minutes
  1. Generate3 minutesEach group writes 8–12 key ideas or terms, one per sticky note.“One idea per note, big enough to read from across the table.”
  2. Link5 minutes“Arrange the notes so every two that touch have a connection. You must be able to say what it is.”
  3. Label2 minutesWrite the connection on a small note or on the paper between them.
  4. Defend3 minutesEach group explains its strongest link to another group or the class.“Which connection surprised you most?”

Watch out for

  • Loose links (“they’re both important”): require a specific verb, such as causes, is part of or leads to.
  • One student arranging: each member must place and explain at least two notes.
  • Too many notes: cap it at 12.

Level up

  • Another group challenges one connection, and you must defend it.
  • Remove one note and explain what breaks.

Why it works

Lists keep ideas separate. Arranging notes forces students to decide how ideas relate and to say why, which builds the connected understanding that lasts longer than isolated facts.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: allow a sketch beside each word.
  • IEP and 504 supports: provide the notes pre-written so students focus on linking.
  • Early finishers: add a new note that links to three others.

Tech option

Use a digital sticky-note board. Paper is faster to move by hand.
