The move

Before a moment where disruptions usually happen, state the expected behavior, have students rehearse it and prompt them as it starts.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 2State it20 seconds
  2. 3Rehearse1 minute
  3. 4Prompt5 seconds
  4. 5Notice it30 seconds

Before class: Name the moment

  1. Name the momentBefore classExample: “moving into groups.”
  2. State it20 seconds“In a minute we’ll move into groups. That looks like: push in your chair, walk, sit, materials out, voices at table level.”
  3. Rehearse1 minute“Let’s practice. Show me, then come back.”Time it if you like.
  4. Prompt5 secondsAs the real moment starts: “Remember: walk, sit, materials out.”
  5. Notice it30 seconds“Table 3 had materials out in 20 seconds. That’s exactly it.”Name the specific behavior.

Why it works

Disruptions cluster at predictable times. Reminding students of the expectation right before the moment (called precorrection), practicing it and then praising students who do it prevents problems instead of reacting to them. This is one of the best-supported classroom management practices.

Watch out for

  • Vague expectations (“be respectful”): describe what it looks and sounds like.
  • Skipping rehearsal: the practice is what makes it stick.
  • Only noticing problems: praise the students who do it right, specifically.

Level up

  • Time the rehearsal and try to beat it.
  • Students write the expectation for the next tricky moment.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: model it physically while you say it.
  • IEP and 504 supports: give a private reminder or a picture card before the group reminder.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyShort model videoRecord students doing the routine well (with permission) and replay it as the precorrection.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. The tricky moment in my class is [transition or moment]. Write the expected behavior as 4-5 short steps, a 20-second script to state it, a 1-minute rehearsal plan and a 5-word prompt for the moment itself. Before you finish: Every step must be something you could see a student do. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
