The move

Once a week, ask students what helped them learn and what got in the way, change one thing, and tell them you did.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 1Hand out slips20 seconds
  2. 2Two questions2 minutes
  3. 5Close the loop30 seconds

Before class: Read

  1. Hand out slips20 secondsNo names.
  2. Two questions2 minutesCollect the slips at the door.“What helped you learn today? What got in the way? Be honest. No names.”
  3. ReadAfter class, 5 minutesSort into piles. Look for patterns, not single comments.
  4. Change one thingNext classPick the most common, doable request.
  5. Close the loop30 seconds“Several of you said the directions were too fast. So today they’re on the board too. Thank you.”

Why it works

Students see your teaching from the other side of the desk. In a large national study, students’ survey ratings of their teachers predicted which classrooms made bigger learning gains. And telling students what you changed shows their voice matters, which makes the next round more honest.

Watch out for

  • Taking one comment personally: look for patterns across many slips.
  • Asking and not acting: students stop answering if nothing changes.
  • Vague answers (“it was fine”): ask about one specific activity.

Level up

  • Let a student suggest the next tweak.
  • Ask about a specific strategy: “Did today’s partner work help? Why or why not?”

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: accept answers in any language, or pictures.
  • IEP and 504 supports: offer a checkbox version.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyAnonymous Google FormTurn off email collection. Responses build a weekly record in a Sheet so you can see whether your changes helped.
  • AugmentGemini for Education (if your district allows it)Paste in the anonymous responses and ask for the top three patterns.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Here are anonymous student comments on what helped and what got in the way: [paste comments, no names]. Group them into themes, count each theme, and suggest the one most doable change I could make next class, with how I would tell students about it. Before you finish: Quote students' words where useful; don't invent comments. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.

Research links
How it helps: Student feedback to the teacher

Practice-based

Kane, T. J., & Staiger, D. O. (2012). Gathering Feedback for Teaching: Combining High-Quality Observations with Student Surveys and Achievement Gains. Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, Measures of Effective Teaching (MET) Project. Student survey ratings predicted teachers’ achievement gains. That study used formal surveys; weekly feedback slips are a classroom-scale version.
