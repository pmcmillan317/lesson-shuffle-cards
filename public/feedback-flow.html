The move

Drafts stay on desks while students rotate, reading a classmate’s work each round with one focus question and leaving one specific comment.

See how it works

At a glance

Time in class
15 minutes
Prep
2 minutes: write 3 focus questions, one per round.
Materials
Student drafts; sticky notes or pens
Grouping
Individuals rotating
Use it when
Students have a draft: writing, a lab report, a design, a solution

Why it works

Feedback helps most when it answers three questions: Where am I going? How am I doing? What’s next? A single focus question per round keeps comments specific instead of “Good job!”, and reading others’ work shows students what quality looks like.

Step by step

In class: about 12 min

  1. 1Set up1 minute
  2. 2Round 14 minutes
  3. 3Rounds 2 and 34 minutes each
  4. 4Return and read2 minutes
  5. 5Act1 minute
  1. Set up1 minuteDrafts on desks, a blank sticky note or comment sheet beside each.
  2. Round 14 minutes“Move one desk to your right. Read. Focus question: Is the main idea clear? Leave one comment that starts ‘I noticed…’ or ‘Try…’”
  3. Rounds 2 and 34 minutes eachRotate again with a new focus question each time.
  4. Return and read2 minutesStudents go back to their own draft and read all the comments.
  5. Act1 minute“Circle the one comment you’ll use. Start revising now.”

See it in action

Story drafts. Focus: “Does it have a beginning, middle and end?”

Lab conclusions. Focus: “Is there evidence from the data?”

Thesis paragraphs. Focus: “Could someone argue against this claim?”

Watch out for

  • Vague comments: require a starter and a specific line or spot.
  • Hurtful comments: model kind, specific and helpful before round 1.
  • Readers who don’t finish: keep drafts short or have them read one section.

Level up

  • Authors pick the comment that changed their draft most.
  • Readers suggest a rewrite of one sentence.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: post comment starters; allow comments in home language plus English.
  • IEP and 504 supports: provide a checklist version of the focus questions.
  • Early finishers: leave a second comment on a different focus.

Tech option

Students rotate through shared docs and leave comments instead of sticky notes.

Evidence

Moderate

Hattie, J., & Timperley, H. (2007). The power of feedback. Review of Educational Research, 77(1), 81–112. Feedback works best when it is specific to the task and tells the learner what to do next.
