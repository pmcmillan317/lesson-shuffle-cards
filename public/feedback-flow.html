Feedback Flow

Is peer feedback “good job”?

15 min2-min prepModerate
StepsAt a glanceDiagramExamplesGo deeper

The move

“Good job” isn’t feedback. Drafts stay on desks. Students move one desk over, read the draft, and answer a question you post (like “Is the claim clear?”) on a sticky note. Run three rounds with a new question each time. Authors read every note and revise.

Step by step

Step timing

  5. 5Revise3 minutes
  1. Set up1 minuteDrafts stay on desks. Put three blank sticky notes beside each draft (or use the comment sheet). Post your three focus questions where you will reveal one per round.“‘Good job’ isn’t feedback. Each round you’ll answer one question about the draft in front of you, and be specific.”
  2. Round 13 minutesPost the first question. Students move one desk over, read the draft, and answer the question on a sticky note.“Move one desk to your right. Read. Question: Is the claim clear? Answer it on a sticky note.”
  3. Rounds 2 and 33 minutes eachSame steps. Post a new question each round, and students move one more desk.“Move one desk again. New question: [your question].”
  4. Authors read every note2 minutesStudents go back to their own draft and read every note.
  5. Revise3 minutesStudents use the notes to improve their draft.“Use the notes to make your draft stronger. Start revising now.”

Why it works

Feedback helps most when it answers three questions: Where am I going? How am I doing? What’s next? A single focus question per round keeps comments specific instead of “Good job!”, and reading others’ work shows students what quality looks like.

Watch out for

  • Vague comments: ask readers to answer the posted question and point to a specific line or spot.
  • Hurtful comments: model kind, specific and helpful before round 1.
  • Readers who don’t finish: keep drafts short or have them read one section.

Level up

  • Authors pick the comment that changed their draft most.
  • Readers suggest a rewrite of one sentence.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: post comment starters; allow comments in home language plus English.
  • IEP and 504 supports: provide a checklist version of the focus questions.
  • Early finishers: leave a second comment on a different focus.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyGoogle Docs comments and suggesting modeReaders leave comments tied to the exact sentence. Writers resolve each one, and version history shows what they actually revised.
  • ModifyRotation roster in Google SheetsLink every draft in a Sheet; each round, students open the next link down. No papers move, and every draft gets three readers.
  • AugmentBrisk Teaching (free tier)Speed up your own comments on the final draft so students get feedback while it still matters.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. I am running a feedback rotation on [type of draft]. Write 3 focus questions, one per round, each about one feature of strong work. For each question give two example sticky-note answers: one vague (like "Good job") and one specific. Before you finish: Each focus question should be answerable by a classmate in 3 minutes. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.

Evidence

Moderate

Hattie, J., & Timperley, H. (2007). The power of feedback. Review of Educational Research, 77(1), 81–112. Feedback works best when it is specific to the task and tells the learner what to do next.

Double, K. S., McGrane, J. A., & Hopfenbeck, T. N. (2020). The impact of peer assessment on academic performance: A meta-analysis of control group studies. Educational Psychology Review, 32(2), 481–509. Peer assessment raised achievement compared with no assessment and with teacher assessment alone.
