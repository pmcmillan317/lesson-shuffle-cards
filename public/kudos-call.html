The move

Make two-minute positive calls to two families a week about a student’s specific effort or improvement, and log them so every student in one class gets a call before you start the next class.

Why it works

Most calls home bring bad news. A good-news call builds trust with families and makes later conversations easier. In a randomized study, regular teacher calls and messages to families increased homework completion and on-task behavior.

Watch out for

  • Calling only top students: follow the log through one class roster before starting the next.
  • Too many students to reach: with several sections, start with the class that needs it most. At two calls a week, a class of 28 takes about a semester.
  • Sneaking in a concern: keep good-news calls 100% positive.
  • Language barriers: use your district’s interpretation service or a translated message.

Level up

  • Let the student listen in on speaker.
  • Have students nominate a classmate for a call.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual families: use an interpreter or a translated text message.
  • IEP and 504 supports: share progress toward their goals.
  • Families who are hard to reach: try a text or note home.

Tech option

Your district’s messaging app works when calls aren’t possible; keep the message specific.

Evidence

Moderate

Kraft, M. A., & Dougherty, S. M. (2013). The effect of teacher-family communication on student engagement: Evidence from a randomized field experiment. Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness, 6(3), 199–222. Frequent teacher-to-family contact improved homework completion, on-task behavior and participation (in a summer program for middle and high school students).
