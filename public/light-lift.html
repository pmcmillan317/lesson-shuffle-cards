The move

Once a week, spend five minutes on a quick, low-stakes game so students get comfortable taking risks in front of each other.

    A student says three statements about themselves; two are true. The class guesses the lie. Say: “Tell us three things about you. Two true, one made up. We’ll guess the fake.”

    Read a choice. Students point to one side of the room or show A/B fingers, and one or two explain why. Say: “Would you rather be able to fly or be invisible? Point left for fly, right for invisible.”

Watch out for

  • Games that drag: five minutes, then stop.
  • Personal questions: keep prompts light; never require a student to share.
  • The same volunteers: rotate who goes first.

Level up

  • Students take turns running the game and writing the prompts.
  • Make the prompts about today’s content.

Why it works

Students try hard problems and share half-formed ideas only when they feel safe with the people around them. Short, playful moments build that trust quickly and cost very little class time.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: Would You Rather works with pointing and pictures.
  • IEP and 504 supports: let students pass or write their statements instead of saying them.

Tech option

Show the Would You Rather choice with two pictures on a slide.
