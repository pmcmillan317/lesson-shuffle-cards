The move

Students use today’s concept to propose a fix for a real problem nearby.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 1Pose the problem1 minute
  2. 2Connect1 minute
  3. 3Plan6–10 minutes
  4. 4Pitch1 minute per group
  5. 5Close1 minute
  1. Pose the problem1 minute(Use a real number if you have one, or have students estimate.)“Our cafeteria throws away about 40 pounds of food a day. Using what we learned today, how could we cut that?”
  2. Connect1 minute“Which idea from today’s lesson helps here?”
  3. Plan6–10 minutesGroups work through four boxes: 1. What’s the problem, in one sentence? 2. Which idea from today applies? 3. What’s the fix? 4. How would we know it worked?
  4. Pitch1 minute per groupGroups share. The class asks one question each.
  5. Close1 minute“What did you have to understand about today’s concept to make that work?”

Why it works

Using an idea on a real problem shows students why it matters and gives them a reason to understand it well enough to apply it. Tasks that mirror real-world use build knowledge that transfers beyond the worksheet.

Watch out for

  • Problems too big (“end hunger”): keep it local and small.
  • Fixes that skip the concept: require groups to name the idea they used, in box 2.
  • Time creep: run a visible timer.

Level up

  • Send the best fix to someone who can act on it.
  • Groups estimate the cost or impact of their fix.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: allow a labeled drawing of the solution.
  • IEP and 504 supports: give a three-box planning frame (Problem / Concept / Fix).
  • Early finishers: predict one way their fix could fail.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • RedefineGoogle Forms + Sheets surveyStudents survey the school to collect real data on the problem, then chart it in Sheets. Their fix rests on numbers they gathered themselves.
  • ModifyGoogle My MapsMap where the problem happens (litter hot spots, crossing times) with layers, so the pattern is visible.
  • RedefineGoogle SitesPublish the proposals and send the link to someone who can act on them: the principal, the PTA or a town office.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. List 5 real, local-scale problems a [grade] class could tackle using [concept], like waste in the cafeteria or traffic at pickup. For each: the problem in one sentence, how [concept] applies, what data students could collect themselves, and what a strong proposal would include. Before you finish: Only suggest data students can actually collect at school. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
