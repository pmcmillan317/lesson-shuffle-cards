The move

Spend ten minutes of a planning period in a colleague’s class and leave with one move to try this week.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 1Pick a focus1 minute
  2. 2Ask1 minute
  3. 3Visit10 minutes
  4. 4Pick one move2 minutes
  5. 5Thank them1 minute
  1. Pick a focus1 minuteOne question, such as “How does she get groups started so fast?”
  2. Ask1 minute“Could I pop in for ten minutes during my planning on Tuesday? I want to see how you start group work. Not an evaluation, just stealing ideas.”
  3. Visit10 minutesSit at the side. Watch for your focus only. Jot what the teacher says and does.
  4. Pick one move2 minutesWrite it down in one sentence.
  5. Thank them1 minuteA quick note naming what you learned.
  6. Try itThis weekTry your one move in a class this week. Note what happened.

Why it works

Seeing a technique in a real classroom shows details no description captures. A short, focused visit with no evaluation is low-pressure for both teachers, and professional learning works best when it’s tied to your own classroom practice.

Watch out for

  • Watching everything: one focus question only.
  • Turning it into feedback for them: you’re there to learn, not to evaluate.
  • No time: 10 minutes counts. You don’t need a full period.

Level up

  • Invite that teacher to watch you try it.
  • Visit a teacher in a different grade or subject.

Make it work for everyone

  • Choose a focus that helps a student group you’re struggling to reach.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyShared Google Form + SheetEvery visitor logs the one move they took. The staff builds a searchable bank of moves from each other’s rooms.
  • ModifyGoogle Calendar appointment scheduleColleagues post open-door times so visits are easy to book.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. I want to see how a colleague handles [focus]. Write a short, friendly ask, 3 look-fors for a 10-minute visit and a thank-you note template that names one move I will try. Before you finish: Make it clear this is not an evaluation. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.

How it helps: Teacher learning from colleagues

Practice-based

Timperley, H. (2008). Teacher Professional Learning and Development (Educational Practices Series 18). International Academy of Education. Professional learning works best when it’s focused on specific student needs and connected to classroom practice.

Darling-Hammond, L., Wei, R. C., Andree, A., Richardson, N., & Orphanos, S. (2009). Professional Learning in the Learning Profession. National Staff Development Council. Finds that effective professional learning is sustained, focused on content and connected to teachers’ own classrooms.
