Draw it to know it

The move

Students draw the idea you just taught, using boxes, arrows and labels, then explain their sketch to a partner.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 2Set the rule15 seconds
  2. 4Sketch2 minutes
  3. 5Explain to a partner1 minute each
  4. 6Share two1 minute
  1. Teach firstSketching works best right after students have heard or read the idea.
  2. Set the rule15 seconds“Stick figures are fine. This isn’t art class. Use boxes, arrows and labels to show how it works.”
  3. Clear the boardErase or hide your own diagram so students can’t copy it.
  4. Sketch2 minutesSet a visible timer.
  5. Explain to a partner1 minute each“Walk your partner through your sketch. Partner, ask one question about it.”
  6. Share two1 minuteShow two sketches that took different approaches and ask what each one shows well.

See how it works

See it in action

The life cycle of a butterfly, or the beginning, middle and end of a story.

The water cycle, or how a bill becomes a law.

How a chemical bond forms, or the causes of World War I as a web of arrows.

Sketch a word problem before solving it.

Watch out for

  • Students making art instead of meaning: keep the timer short and say “no color needed.”
  • “I can’t draw”: boxes, arrows and labels count. Show a quick, messy example of your own.
  • Copying your diagram: clear the board before they start.

Level up

  • Partners guess the concept before the artist explains it.
  • Add a second panel showing what happens next, or what changes if one part is removed.

Variation: build it in 3D

For ideas with shape or structure (molecules, landforms, geometric solids), hand out a pinch of clay or a square of foil instead of paper. Same steps, same timer.

Why it works

Turning words into a picture forces students to decide what matters most and how the parts connect. Research on learning by drawing finds that creating your own sketch of an explanation helps students understand and remember it, especially when the drawing focuses on how things work rather than how they look.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: drawing lowers the language load. Let students label in any language, then add key English terms.
  • IEP and 504 supports: give a partly drawn frame (empty boxes and arrows) to fill in.
  • Early finishers: make the “what happens next” panel.

Tech option

Students can sketch in Google Drawings or a Canva whiteboard and share to a class board.
