The move

Students take rotating classroom or group roles, each with a one-line job description, so you stop running every task yourself.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 2Assign1 minute
  2. 4RotateWeekly, 1 minute

Before class: Choose 4–6 roles

  1. Choose 4–6 rolesOncePost each with a one-line job. Starter set:
    • Materials Manager: hands out and collects supplies.
    • Tech Helper: helps classmates log in and troubleshoot.
    • Discussion Captain: makes sure everyone in the group speaks.
    • Timekeeper: watches the clock and gives 2-minute warnings.
    • Recorder: writes down the group’s answer.
  2. Assign1 minute“Check the chart for your job this week. Read your one line.”
  3. Hand offOngoingWhen a job comes up, send it to the role.“Materials Managers, you’re up.”
  4. RotateWeekly, 1 minuteMove every name down one role.

Why it works

Clear roles give every student a reason to contribute and make each person responsible for part of the work. Cooperative learning research finds that individual accountability like this is a key ingredient when groups work well. You also get time back.

Watch out for

  • Doing the job yourself: wait for the student, even if it’s slower at first.
  • Vague roles: one line, one verb.
  • Unfair rotation: track it on the chart so everyone gets every job.

Level up

  • Students apply for the role they want with a one-sentence pitch.
  • Students invent a new role the class needs.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: add a picture to each role.
  • IEP and 504 supports: match first roles to strengths, then rotate.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • AugmentGoogle Sheets role chartA simple formula rotates every name down one role each week, so you never redo the chart.
  • ModifyRole how-to videos on a Google SiteEach role holder records a 60-second how-to for the next person. Students train each other.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Write job descriptions for 6 classroom roles in a [grade] class, one line each, plus a 60-second training script for each role that the current holder could teach the next one. Before you finish: Every job must be something a student can actually do without me. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.

Build a classroom app

Build a single-file web app (HTML, CSS and JavaScript in one file) for my [grade] class. Make a weekly job board: I paste student first names and a list of roles; it shows who has which job this week in big cards, and a "Next week" button rotates everyone down one role. Requirements: no sign-in, no outside services, nothing saved except in this browser; large text that reads on a projector; works on a Chromebook; every control reachable by keyboard; clean, friendly design. Show me the working app, then tell me how to change the content.

Works in Gemini Canvas, ChatGPT Canvas or Claude. Each gives you a working app you can preview, tweak by asking for changes and share by link.
