The move

Students write as many questions as they can about a prompt, without answering any, then pick the one worth investigating.

Step by step

Step timing

  2. 2State the rules30 seconds
  3. 3Storm5 minutes
  4. 4Sort1 minute
  5. 5Choose1 minute
  1. Show the prompt30 seconds“Look closely. In a minute you’ll write questions, only questions.”
  2. State the rules30 secondsWrite as many questions as you can. Don’t stop to discuss or answer. Write every question exactly as it’s said.
  3. Storm5 minutesOne recorder per group writes.“Go! Aim for 15 questions.”
  4. Sort1 minuteMark each question C (closed: one-word answer) or O (open: needs explaining).
  5. Choose1 minuteEach group shares its pick.“Circle the one question that would be worth a whole lesson.”
  6. Use the questionsOver the unitPost each group’s chosen question. Answer at least one in a later lesson and point back to it: “Remember this question? Today we can answer it.”

Why it works

Asking questions makes students active. When they generate and choose their own questions, they care more about the answers and you learn what they already know and wonder.

Watch out for

  • Students answering each other: remind them “questions only” and keep the clock running.
  • Statements disguised as questions: ask the recorder to rewrite them as questions.
  • Stalling: give a starter: “Why…? What if…? How does…?”

Level up

  • Groups pick the one question worth a whole lesson.
  • Rewrite one closed question as an open one, and the reverse.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: post question starters in English and home languages.
  • IEP and 504 supports: let students say questions while a partner writes.
  • Early finishers: rank their top three questions.

Tech option

Groups type questions into a shared doc so the whole class sees them.
