The move

Each pair learns one subtopic fast, then teaches it to the class in exactly two minutes.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 1Assign1 minute
  2. 2Research10 minutes
  3. 4Present2 minutes each
  1. Assign1 minuteGive each pair a subtopic and one guiding question.
  2. Research10 minutes“Find the three things your classmates most need to know. You’ll have two minutes. Not one second more.”
  3. PlanIncluded abovePairs decide who says what. Notes on one index card only.
  4. Present2 minutes eachStart the timer. When it beeps, they stop mid-sentence.“Time! Big applause.”
  5. CaptureOngoingThe audience writes one takeaway per talk.

At a glance

Time in class
About 15 minutes for 5–6 pairs (10 research + 2 per talk). Run talks over two days for bigger classes.
Prep
5 minutes: list one subtopic per pair.
Materials
Textbook, articles or devices; a timer
Grouping
Pairs
Use it when
A topic splits into parts students can learn on their own

See how it works

See it in action

Each pair teaches one animal’s habitat.

Each pair teaches one of the original 13 colonies.

Each pair teaches one cause of the 2008 financial crisis.

Watch out for

  • Reading from notes: one index card only.
  • Overruns: the timer is the game. Stop them every time.
  • A passive audience: require one takeaway per talk.

Level up

  • The audience asks one question per talk, and presenters answer without notes.
  • Cut the time to 90 seconds.

Why it works

Preparing to explain something to others makes students organize and question what they’ve learned, and explaining it out loud deepens it further. The hard time limit forces them to choose what matters most.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: allow one visual and a sentence frame: “The most important thing about ___ is…”
  • IEP and 504 supports: offer a shorter text and let the partner do more of the speaking.
  • Early finishers: prepare a one-question quiz for their talk.

Tech option

Pairs can make one slide. No more.

Evidence

Review of Educational Research, 77(4), 534–574. Students learn by explaining, especially when they reflect on and build on what they know rather than just repeating it.
