The move

Pick one daily routine, ask students what slows it down and what would make it fairer, and test their best idea for a week.

  1. Name the routine1 minute“Our turn-in routine takes forever. Let’s fix it together.”
  2. Spot the friction2 minutes“What slows this down? What feels unfair?”Chart answers.
  3. Brainstorm fixes3 minutes“How could we make it faster, fairer or clearer?”Chart 3–4 ideas.
  4. Choose one1 minuteVote, or you pick the most realistic.
  5. Post and practice2 minutesWrite the new step in one sentence and run it once.
  6. Trial for a weekDailyUse the new version every day.
  7. Judge it1 minute, Friday“Did this save time? Keep, tweak or toss?”

Why it works

Clear, practiced routines save instructional time and prevent problems. Asking students to redesign one gives them ownership, so they’re more likely to follow the new version, and they often spot friction you don’t see.

Watch out for

  • Changing too many routines: one at a time.
  • Ideas you can’t use: set limits up front (“must take under a minute, no extra cost”).
  • Judging after one day: give it the full week.

Level up

  • Students run the trial and judge it.
  • A student team times the routine before and after.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: post the new routine with a picture for each step.
  • IEP and 504 supports: check that the new routine works for students with mobility or attention needs.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyGoogle Sheets before-and-after timingTime the routine for a week before and after the change, so “keep, tweak or toss” rests on data.
  • AugmentGoogle Forms or MentimeterAnonymous friction survey and a quick vote on fixes.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Our [routine] is slow. Write 4 questions to ask students about what slows it down and what feels unfair, then give me 4 likely fixes and how I would measure whether each saved time. Before you finish: Every fix must be testable in one week. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
