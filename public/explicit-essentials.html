The move

You model the skill while thinking aloud, students try it with you, then they try it alone.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 1Show it: I do3–4 minutes
  2. 2Share it: we do3–5 minutes
  3. 3Check1 minute
  4. 4Release: you do3–5 minutes
  1. Show it: I do3–4 minutesWork one example while saying your thinking out loud, including how you check your work.“Watch my pencil and listen to my thinking. First I notice…”
  2. Share it: we do3–5 minutesDo a second example together. Students tell you each step. On a third, start it and let students finish the last steps.“What do I do first? Tell your partner, then tell me.”
  3. Check1 minuteGive one quick question and look at every answer (thumbs, boards or paper). Release when about 4 in 5 get it right. If fewer do, run another “we do.”
  4. Release: you do3–5 minutesStudents try two or three alone. Walk the room. Look at each student’s first answer. Send anyone stuck to your side table.“Now you try. I’ll come around.”

Why it works

New learners can’t hold many steps in their heads at once. Seeing an expert do the skill step by step, then practicing with support before working alone, keeps the load manageable and catches mistakes early. This “I do, we do, you do” pattern is backed by Rosenshine’s review of effective teaching.

Watch out for

  • Silent modeling: say every thought out loud, especially the checks.
  • Skipping “we do”: it’s where you catch mistakes before they set.
  • Releasing too soon: wait until about 4 in 5 students get the check question right. Students who practice errors alone practice the errors.

Level up

  • Hand the marker to a student to model the next one.
  • Model a mistake on purpose and have students catch it.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: post the steps with a picture or symbol for each.
  • IEP and 504 supports: leave the worked example on the board during “you do.”
  • Early finishers: write and solve their own problem.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyScreen recorder (Loom, free for verified educators, or Screencastify)Record your I-do once and post it in Classroom. Students rewatch the worked example during you-do while you coach the ones who need you.
  • ModifyWhiteboard.fi (free plan)Every student solves the check problem on a private board you see live. Release when about 4 in 5 boards are right.
  • ModifyGoogle Forms with branchingRight on the check goes to independent practice; wrong goes to a section with a second worked example. Each student gets the next step they need.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Plan an I do / we do / you do sequence for [skill]. Give me: one worked example with my think-aloud written word for word, including how I check my answer; two we-do problems of rising difficulty; one check question with the 3 most likely wrong answers and what each reveals; and four you-do problems. Before you finish: Solve every problem yourself and fix any error before showing me. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
