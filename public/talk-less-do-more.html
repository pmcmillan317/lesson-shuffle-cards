The move

You cap your own talking at five minutes at a time, then hand the work to students.

Step by step

  1. Plan your fiveBefore classSplit what students must hear into small steps. Each step gets up to five minutes, then student practice on that step. Five minutes is a starting point. Adjust it to your class.
  2. Start the timerWhen you start talking“I have five minutes. Watch the clock with me.”
  3. TeachUp to 5 minutesWhen the buzzer goes, stop, even mid-sentence.
  4. Hand it offStudents practice exactly what you just taught: one problem, one question or one sentence.“That’s my time. Your turn.”
  5. Next stepCheck the practice, then teach the next small step for up to five minutes. Short steps with practice after each tend to work better than one long explanation.

Why it works

Students learn by doing the thinking, not by watching you do it. A review of 225 college STEM studies (Freeman et al., 2014) found students in active classes did better than in lecture classes. Most K-12 evidence is less direct. A timer makes your talk time visible and keeps it short.

Watch out for

  • “Just one more thing”: write it down and save it for the next five.
  • Students waiting for you to explain again: answer questions with a question.
  • No task ready: the move only works if students have something to do.

Level up

  • Hand a student the timer and let them call you on it.
  • Cut your talk to three minutes.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: post your key points in writing so they stay after you stop talking.
  • IEP and 504 supports: check in one-on-one during the task.
  • Early finishers: have an extension ready so they don’t wait on you.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyShort screencasts in ClassroomRecord each 5-minute chunk once. Students who need it rewatch while you coach, and absent students get the real lesson.
  • ModifyGoogle Forms one-question checkAfter each chunk, one auto-graded question. The response chart tells you whether to teach the next step or reteach.
  • AugmentClassroomscreenA visible 5:00 timer keeps you honest, and the class learns to watch it with you.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Break this lesson into chunks of 5 minutes or less of teacher talk: [paste plan or objective]. After each chunk, give one practice task students do right away, and the quick check that tells me whether to move on. Before you finish: Count the talk time; no chunk over 5 minutes. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.

Build a classroom app

Build a single-file web app (HTML, CSS and JavaScript in one file) for my [grade] class. Make a "Talk Less" lesson timer: I list chunks with a practice task for each. It runs a 5-minute "Teacher talk" countdown, then switches to the practice task full-screen with its own timer, then the next chunk. Chime at each switch. Requirements: no sign-in, no outside services, nothing saved except in this browser; large text that reads on a projector; works on a Chromebook; every control reachable by keyboard; clean, friendly design. Show me the working app, then tell me how to change the content.

Works in Gemini Canvas, ChatGPT Canvas or Claude. Each gives you a working app you can preview, tweak by asking for changes and share by link.
