The move

You cap your own talking at five minutes at a time, then hand the work to students.

Step by step

  1. Plan your fiveBefore classDecide the one or two things students must hear from you. Everything else goes in the task.
  2. Start the timerWhen you start talking“I have five minutes. Watch the clock with me.”
  3. TeachUp to 5 minutesWhen the buzzer goes, stop, even mid-sentence.
  4. Hand it off“That’s my time. Your turn.”Launch the task.
  5. Restart only if neededIf you must teach more, take another five later, not now.

See how it works

See it in action

Explain one phonics pattern in five minutes, then students sort word cards.

Introduce slope in five minutes, then pairs find slopes on real staircases or ramps in photos.

Set the historical context in five minutes, then students analyze a primary source.

Watch out for

  • “Just one more thing”: write it down and save it for the next five.
  • Students waiting for you to explain again: answer questions with a question.
  • No task ready: the move only works if students have something to do.

Level up

  • Hand a student the timer and let them call you on it.
  • Cut your talk to three minutes.

Why it works

Students learn by doing the thinking, not by watching you do it. Large reviews of classroom research find that students in active lessons outperform those in mostly lecture lessons. A timer makes your talk time visible and keeps it short.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: post your key points in writing so they stay after you stop talking.
  • IEP and 504 supports: check in one-on-one during the task.
  • Early finishers: have an extension ready so they don’t wait on you.

Tech option
