The move

Write one measurable goal for your own teaching with a date attached, and check it weekly.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 1Pick one focus2 minutes
  2. 2Write it3 minutes
  3. 3Calendar it2 minutes
  4. 4TrackWeekly, 2 minutes
  1. Pick one focus2 minutesAsk: “What one change would most help my students right now?”
  2. Write it3 minutesUse: “By [date], I will [action] [how often].” Example: “By December 1, I will use a whiteboard check three times a week.”
  3. Calendar it2 minutesAdd a 2-minute weekly check-in to your calendar.
  4. TrackWeekly, 2 minutesTally how often you did it and one thing you noticed in student work.
  5. Close itOn the dateMet it? Make it a habit and pick a new goal. Not yet? Shrink it.

Why it works

Specific, challenging goals with feedback beat “do your best,” and tracking progress keeps them alive. Choosing just one keeps it realistic in a busy week.

Watch out for

  • Goals you can’t count: “be more engaging” becomes “use one partner-talk move per lesson.”
  • Too many goals: one at a time.
  • No check-in: the calendar reminder is what makes it happen.

Level up

  • Tell a colleague and ask them to check on you.
  • Pull a card from this deck to choose your goal.

Make it work for everyone

  • Choose goals that help the students who are hardest to reach.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • AugmentGoogle Calendar and KeepA recurring 2-minute check-in on your calendar and a Keep checklist for the habit.
  • ModifyGoogle Forms + Sheets tallyLog each time you did it from your phone; a chart shows whether the habit is sticking.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. I want to improve [teaching habit]. Help me write one goal in the form "By [date], I will [action] [how often]", a 2-minute weekly check-in, and what evidence in student work would show it is helping. Before you finish: Keep the goal small enough to do this week. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
