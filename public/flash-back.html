The move

Twice a week, open class with three recall questions: one from last week, one from last month and one from last unit.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 2Answer from memory2 minutes
  2. 3Reveal1–2 minutes
  3. 4Quick poll30 seconds

Before class: Post three questions

  1. Post three questionsBefore classLabel them: Last week. Last month. Last unit. Pick questions most students can answer with some effort; if almost no one gets one, reteach it before asking again.
  2. Answer from memory2 minutes“No notes. Answer all three. Guess if you’re not sure.”
  3. Reveal1–2 minutesShow answers. Students check and correct in a different color.
  4. Quick poll30 secondsTally each on the board. Note which one the class forgot.“Hands up if you got last week’s. Last month’s. Last unit’s.”

Why it works

We forget fast when a unit moves on. Recalling older material at spaced intervals interrupts that forgetting and makes memories last much longer than cramming. Mixing old and new also shows students the course connects.

Watch out for

  • Only asking yesterday’s content: the older questions are the point.
  • Grading it: keep it low stakes.
  • Questions too long: one-line answers.

Level up

  • Students write the flashback questions for each other.
  • Add a fourth question from last semester.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: keep vocabulary in questions familiar; allow sketches.
  • IEP and 504 supports: offer multiple-choice versions.
  • Early finishers: explain how two of the answers connect.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyGoogle Forms quiz from a question bankAuto-grade the three questions. The response Sheet shows which distance back (week, month, unit) the class forgets most.
  • AugmentFlippity flashcards from a Google SheetOne Sheet that grows all year becomes instant flashcards for spaced review.
  • ModifyQuizlet or Gimkit assignmentsSpaced practice at home on last month’s and last unit’s content.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Here are topics from last week, last month and last unit: [list]. Write 3 recall questions for each time period, short-answer only, with answers. Most students should be able to answer them with some effort. Before you finish: Avoid questions that can be answered by guessing. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.

Build a classroom app

Build a single-file web app (HTML, CSS and JavaScript in one file) for my [grade] class. Make a "Flash Back" warm-up app: I keep a growing bank of questions tagged by date taught. Each day it pulls one question from about a week ago, one from about a month ago and one from the oldest unit, shows them together, then reveals answers on click. Requirements: no sign-in, no outside services, nothing saved except in this browser; large text that reads on a projector; works on a Chromebook; every control reachable by keyboard; clean, friendly design. Show me the working app, then tell me how to change the content.

Works in Gemini Canvas, ChatGPT Canvas or Claude. Each gives you a working app you can preview, tweak by asking for changes and share by link.
