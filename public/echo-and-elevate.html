Echo & Elevate

Answers nobody else hears

3–5 minNo prepModerate
StepsAt a glanceDiagramExamplesGo deeper
Tap to enlarge

The move

When one student answers, call on a second student to restate it in their own words. The first speaker confirms. Then a third student builds on it or challenges it.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 2Ask a question10 seconds
  2. 3Echo30 seconds
  3. 4Confirm10 seconds
  4. 5Elevate30 seconds
  5. 6Keep the chain2–3 minutes

Before class (step 1): Post the starters (optional)

  1. Post the starters (optional)Once“So you’re saying…” and “I’d add… / I disagree because…” Posting them helps, but you don’t need them to start.
  2. Ask a question10 secondsAsk a whole-class question and take one answer.
  3. Echo30 secondsCall on a second student to restate it in their own words.“Who can say what Maya said, in your own words?”
  4. Confirm10 secondsThe first speaker tells the class if that is what they meant.“Maya, is that what you meant?”
  5. Elevate30 secondsCall on a third student to build on the idea or challenge it.“Who can add to that, or disagree?”
  6. Keep the chain2–3 minutesRepeat: echo, confirm, then elevate. Stay quiet and let students pass it along.

Why it works

When only one student talks, the rest can tune out. Asking students to restate a classmate’s idea makes everyone listen, and building on it turns separate answers into a real discussion. These “talk moves” come from research on productive classroom discussion.

Watch out for

  • Word-for-word repeating: the echo must be in the student’s own words. That’s the thinking.
  • The same three voices: use name sticks (see Every Hand Up) to choose who echoes.
  • Challenges that feel personal: model “I disagree with the idea because…”

Level up

  • Keep the chain going five students deep, no teacher talk.
  • The original speaker gets the last word: “What changed in your thinking?”

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: starters on the board in English and home languages.
  • IEP and 504 supports: let a student echo to a partner first, then to the class.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyGoogle Classroom question post with replies onStudents answer, then reply to a classmate starting with “So you’re saying…” and adding one idea. Everyone echoes and elevates, not just three people.
  • ModifyGoogle Docs discussionRun the chain as comment threads on one shared question. Version history shows who built on whom.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Write 5 open questions on [topic] that have more than one good answer. For each, give a likely first answer, a strong paraphrase of it, and two ways a student could build on it or respectfully disagree. Before you finish: Every question must have at least two defensible answers. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
