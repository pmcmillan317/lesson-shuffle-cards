Echo & Elevate

Say it back, then build

The move

After a student answers, a second student restates it in their own words, then a third builds on it or challenges it.

Why it works

When only one student talks, the rest can tune out. Asking students to restate a classmate’s idea makes everyone listen, and building on it turns separate answers into a real discussion. These “talk moves” come from research on productive classroom discussion.

Step by step

In class: about 3–5 min

  1. 2Ask an open question10 seconds
  2. 3Echo30 seconds
  3. 4Elevate30 seconds
  4. 5Keep the chain2–3 minutes

Before class: Post the starters

  1. Post the startersOnce“So you’re saying…” and “I’d add… / I disagree because…”
  2. Ask an open question10 secondsTake one answer.
  3. Echo30 seconds“Who can say what Maya said, in your own words?”Check with Maya: “Is that what you meant?”
  4. Elevate30 seconds“Who can add to that, or disagree?”
  5. Keep the chain2–3 minutesRepeat: echo, then elevate.

See it in action

“Why did the character lie?” Echo, then add a reason.

“Is this a fair trade?” Echo, then challenge with evidence.

“Was the treaty a success?” Echo, then build with a second source.

Watch out for

  • Word-for-word repeating: the echo must be in the student’s own words. That’s the thinking.
  • The same three voices: use name sticks (see Every Hand Up) to choose who echoes.
  • Challenges that feel personal: model “I disagree with the idea because…”

Level up

  • Keep the chain going five students deep without you saying a word.
  • The original speaker gets the last word: “What changed in your thinking?”

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: starters on the board in English and home languages.
  • IEP and 504 supports: let a student echo to a partner first, then to the class.
