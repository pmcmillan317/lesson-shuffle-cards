Hidden Hero

Spot the unsung hero

About 1 minNo prepModerate
StepsDiagramExamplesResourcesEvidence

The move

Find a student you rarely recognize and give them one sincere compliment that names something specific they did.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 3Name the action10 seconds
  2. 5Log it5 seconds

Before class: Scan your roster

  1. Scan your roster30 seconds, before classPut a dot by students you haven’t recognized lately. Pick one.
  2. Watch for a momentDuring classEffort, a good question, helping someone, a strategy, improvement.
  3. Name the action10 seconds“Jordan, you went back and fixed your units without being asked. That’s exactly what scientists do.”
  4. Keep it low-keyQuietly at their desk works for most students, especially older ones.
  5. Log it5 secondsCheck the name off so you rotate through everyone.

See how it works

See it in action

“You sounded out that whole word by yourself. I heard every sound.”

“You asked a question nobody else thought of. It made the whole group think.”

“Your second draft fixed the exact thing we talked about. That’s real revision.”

Watch out for

  • Generic praise (“Good job!”): always name the specific action.
  • Praising ability (“You’re a natural”): praise effort, strategy or choices instead.
  • Public praise that embarrasses: go private for students who don’t like attention.

Level up

  • Catch a hidden hero in every class period.
  • Write a two-line note so they can take it home.

Why it works

Some students go weeks without hearing anything specific about their work. Recognition that names an action (“You checked every step twice”) tells students what to keep doing and builds the relationship. Praise aimed at the person (“You’re so smart”) is far less useful, so the key is naming what they did.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: keep it simple and pair it with a smile or thumbs up.
  • IEP and 504 supports: recognize progress toward their goals, not just grade-level work.

Tech option

A checklist column in your gradebook or roster spreadsheet.

Evidence
