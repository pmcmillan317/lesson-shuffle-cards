Deal Me In

Same groups, same voices

The move

Hand each student a playing card at the door. Group by suit, split the class by color, or call a number, such as “All the sevens, share your answer.” Collect the cards and reshuffle for tomorrow.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 3Group the class30 seconds
  2. 4Call who answers10 seconds
  3. 5Collect and reshuffle1 minute

Before class (step 1): Build the class deck

  1. Build the class deckOnceCount your students. Pull one playing card for each, using each number in all four suits. For 24 students use ace through 6. For 28 use ace through 7. For 32 use ace through 8. With an odd count, add a joker as a wild card that joins any group.
  2. Deal at the doorAs students enterShuffle and hand each student one card. Students keep the card face up on their desk.“Take a card and keep it face up on your desk.”
  3. Group the class30 secondsPick the grouping that fits the task. By suit gives four groups. By color gives two halves.“Find the other students with your suit. That is your group.”“Reds on this side, blacks on that side.”
  4. Call who answers10 secondsCall a number. Each number has one student from every suit, so every group has a voice. Vary the numbers from day to day.“All the sevens, share your answer.”
  5. Collect and reshuffle1 minuteCollect the cards at the door or the end of class. Reshuffle so tomorrow’s groups are new.“Hand your card back as you leave.”

At a glance

Time in class
1–3 minutes each time you group the class or choose who answers.
Prep
5 minutes: build one deck per class period to match the class size, then keep it in a bag or rubber band.
Materials
A deck of playing cards for each class. Tracking chart under Printables.
Grouping
Suits, colors or numbers, then whole class.
Use it when
Picking groups or choosing who answers takes time and never feels fair.

See how it works

See it in action

Hand out cards at the door. Hearts meet at the rug, spades at the window, clubs by the door and diamonds by the board.

Reds argue one side of a question and blacks argue the other. Then call “all the queens” to share the best point.

Group by suit for a lab. Then call “all the fives” to report one result for each group.

Why it works

A card pulled at random takes the arguing out of who picks groups and who answers. Students cannot pick friends or hide from speaking, and you are not seen choosing, so it feels fair. Regrouping every day mixes who works with whom, and the numbers make sure every group has a voice.

Watch out for

  • Lost or traded cards: count the deck when you collect it and tell students no trading.
  • Groups that are uneven: a group of five is fine. A joker can join any group.
  • Absent students: pull their card before you deal.
  • Calling the same numbers: keep a tally of the numbers you call (see the tracking chart under Printables).
  • Cards left on desks: collect them at the door as students leave.

Level up

  • Draw from a second deck to pick who answers.
  • Let the student holding the ace choose how to group the class: suit, color or number.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: suits, colors and numbers need no language, so every student can follow the grouping.
  • IEP and 504 supports: keep a few cards aside so you can quietly place a student with a supportive partner without announcing it.
  • Early finishers: sort the deck back into suits and numbers for tomorrow.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • AugmentWheel of NamesLoad the numbers in your deck and spin to call who answers.
  • AugmentFlippity random name picker and group makerMake random groups or call a name from your roster Sheet when you do not have a deck.
  • ModifyGoogle Sheets tallyTally each time a student answers. Sort the sheet to see who has not been heard from yet.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject] with [number] students. Show me how to build a standard-card deck for exactly [number] students so I can group by suit, by color and by number with groups as even as possible. Then write 5 short sentence stems I can say to call on a number, such as “All the sevens, share your answer.” Before you finish: Tell me what to do with an odd number of students. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
