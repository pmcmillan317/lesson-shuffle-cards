The move

Each student writes one question and answer on a card; partners quiz each other, swap cards and find a new partner.

Step by step

Step timing

  3. 3Quiz30 seconds

  5. 5Trade5 seconds
  6. 6Repeat5 minutes
  1. Write3 minutes“Write one question from this unit on the front of your card and the answer on the back.”Check a few for accuracy.
  2. Stand up, hand up, pair up10 secondsStudents find a partner with a raised hand.
  3. Quiz30 secondsPartner A reads the question. Partner B answers. A coaches or gives a hint if needed, then shows the answer.
  4. Quiz back30 secondsSwitch roles.
  5. Trade5 seconds“Trade cards! Hand up, find a new partner.”
  6. Repeat5 minutesStudents now quiz with the card they just received.

“What event started the Cold War arms race?”

Watch out for

  • Wrong answers on cards: scan cards as students write; fix mistakes before trading.
  • Chatting instead of quizzing: keep the trade rhythm fast.
  • Too-easy questions: require “why” or “how” questions in round two.

Level up

  • The question that stumps the most partners wins.
  • Students write a harder card for round two.

Why it works

Answering questions from memory (practice testing) is one of the most effective ways to learn. Writing a good question is its own review, and swapping cards means each student answers many questions in a few minutes. You don’t have to write any of them.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: allow a picture on the card.
  • IEP and 504 supports: provide pre-written cards.
  • Early finishers: write a second card.

Tech option

None needed. Moving around is part of the point.
