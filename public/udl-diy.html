The move

Instead of building accommodations one student at a time, offer the whole class a few options up front for how they take in the content and show what they know.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 1Name the goal1 minute
  2. 2Add one way in3 minutes
  3. 3Add one support3 minutes
  4. 4Add one way out3 minutes
  5. 5Offer it1 minute, in class
  1. Name the goal1 minuteWhat must every student learn or show? Keep it fixed.
  2. Add one way in3 minutesOffer the reading as text plus audio (text-to-speech), or text plus a short video or diagram.
  3. Add one support3 minutesA graphic organizer, sentence starters or a checklist, available to anyone.
  4. Add one way out3 minutesTwo or three ways to show learning: write it, draw and label it, or record it.
  5. Offer it1 minute, in class“Same goal for everyone. Pick the tools that help you get there.”

See how it works

See it in action

Retell a story by writing, drawing a comic strip or telling it to a recorder.

Read a science article with or without text-to-speech, using an optional cause-and-effect organizer.

Show understanding of a historical event in a paragraph, an annotated timeline or a 90-second recording.

Watch out for

  • Lowering the goal: options change the path, not the target.
  • Too many choices: two or three is enough.
  • Matching tasks to “learning styles”: that idea isn’t supported by research. Offer options based on interests and needs, and let students choose.

Level up

  • Students pick their option and explain why it fits them.
  • Students suggest a new option for next time.

Why it works

Universal Design for Learning (UDL) plans for the range of learners from the start. Supports like text-to-speech or a graphic organizer help students who need them and many who don’t, without singling anyone out. The goal stays the same for everyone; only the path changes.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: include translation or a bilingual glossary as one of the supports.
  • IEP and 504 supports: UDL doesn’t replace legally required accommodations; it reduces how many students need separate ones.
  • Early finishers: a challenge option with the same goal.

Tech option

Use your device’s built-in read-aloud, speech-to-text and translation tools.
