How is X like Y?

The move

Students explain how today’s concept is like a strange, everyday object, and defend the comparison.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 1Model one30 seconds
  2. 2Pose the clash30 seconds
  3. 3Think time30 seconds
  4. 4Pair up1–2 minutes
  5. 5Share out1–2 minutes
  6. 6Close15 seconds
  1. Model one30 secondsThe first time, think aloud through a pairing yourself: one way they’re alike and where it breaks.“A map is like a recipe: both give steps to get somewhere. It breaks because a recipe has one right order.”
  2. Pose the clash30 secondsName the concept and an unrelated object.“How is a simile like a paperclip? Think on your own first.”
  3. Think time30 secondsSilent thinking. Students may jot a few words.
  4. Pair up1–2 minutes“Tell your partner one way they’re alike, and why. Your partner has to agree or push back.”
  5. Share out1–2 minutesTake three answers. After each, ask the class: “Does that comparison hold up? Where does it break?”
  6. Close15 seconds“So the big idea of a simile is that it connects two things. That’s what we’ll use today.”

Why it works

To compare two things, students have to pull out the deep structure of the concept, not just repeat its definition. Comparing ideas this way (analogy) is one of the main ways people understand something new in terms of something they already know.

Watch out for

  • Surface answers (“they’re both small”): ask “How does that connect to what a ___ actually does?”
  • Stuck pairs: give a sentence starter: “They’re alike because both…”
  • Analogies taken too far: ask where the comparison breaks. That’s often where the best learning is.

Level up

  • Students invent their own odd pairing and stump a partner.
  • Pairs list one way the two are alike and one way they’re different.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: show a picture of the object and allow a sketch or a single word.
  • IEP and 504 supports: offer the starter “They’re alike because both ___.”
  • Early finishers: find a second object that works even better and explain why.

Tech option

Put the object’s picture on a slide. Collect answers on a shared board or poll if you want a record.
