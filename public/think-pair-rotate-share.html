The move

Students think alone, discuss with a partner, then switch to a new partner to push the idea further before sharing with the class.

  1. Ask10 secondsPose an open question.
  2. Think30–60 seconds“Think on your own. Jot a note if it helps.”
  3. Pair1–2 minutes“Share with your shoulder partner. Listen for something you didn’t think of.”
  4. Rotate1–2 minutes“Stand up, find someone new. Share your idea AND your first partner’s.”Or use Clock Partners: “Meet your 6 o’clock.”
  5. Share2 minutesTake three ideas from the room.

Why it works

Think time lets every student form an idea. Talking with a partner tests it, and a second partner brings a fresh angle, so students revise instead of settling on their first answer. Structured peer talk like this is a core part of cooperative learning.

Watch out for

  • Skipping think time: without it, the fast thinkers take over.
  • Slow rotations: count down from 20.
  • Pairs that finish early: give a follow-up: “What’s the best argument against your idea?”

Level up

  • Share your second partner’s idea, not your own.
  • Add a third rotation.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: allow first-language talk in the first pair, English in the second.
  • IEP and 504 supports: provide a sentence frame; let students stay seated and have partners come to them.

Tech option

A random-pairing tool or breakout rooms can handle the rotation online. In the room, standing up is faster.
