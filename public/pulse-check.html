The move

Pause mid-lesson for a 30-second thumbs or 0–5 finger check, then reteach, regroup or move on.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 1Ask5 seconds
  2. 2Signal5 seconds
  3. 3Scan10 seconds
  4. 4Confirm1 minute
  5. 5Decide10 seconds
  1. Ask5 seconds“Thumbs up if you could do this alone, sideways if you’re getting there, down if you’re lost.”Or: “Show 0 to 5 fingers.”
  2. Signal5 seconds“Hold it close to your chest so it’s private.”
  3. Scan10 secondsCount roughly: mostly up, mixed or mostly down.
  4. Confirm1 minuteAsk one quick question everyone answers on paper or a whiteboard (see Boards Up!).
  5. Decide10 secondsMostly right: move on. Mixed: pull the sideways and downs into a small group while others start. Mostly wrong: reteach.

Why it works

Quiet students often hide confusion, and pacing by instinct misses it. A quick signal from everyone gives you a read on the room in seconds. One caution: thumbs show how confident students feel, not what they understand, so confirm with one quick question before you move on.

Watch out for

  • Trusting thumbs alone: confident students can still be wrong. Confirm with a question.
  • Peer pressure: signals close to the chest, or eyes closed.
  • Not acting on it: decide your next move before you ask.

Level up

  • Eyes closed, so nobody copies a friend’s thumb.
  • Students who show a thumbs up explain the step to a sideways neighbor.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: gestures need no language.
  • IEP and 504 supports: use colored cards (green, yellow, red) on desks.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyGoogle Forms with branchingOne check question routes students automatically: confident goes to extension, unsure goes to a hint and more practice.
  • AugmentClassroomscreen traffic lightA quick room signal when thumbs feel too public.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Write 6 quick check questions on [topic] for a mid-lesson pulse check, each answerable in 30 seconds. For each, say what I do next if most get it right, if it's mixed and if most get it wrong. Before you finish: The questions must show real understanding, not just recall. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
