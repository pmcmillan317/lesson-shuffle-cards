Pulse Check

Who’s lost but not saying?

30 secNo prepModerate

The move

Mid-lesson, everyone closes their eyes and shows a thumb: up, sideways or down for how well they’ve got it. Thumbs up keep working. Sideways and down bring their work to one table for a two-minute reteach.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 1Ask10 seconds
  2. 2Show10 seconds
  3. 3Scan10 seconds
  4. 4Decide10 seconds
  1. Ask10 seconds“Thumbs up if you could do this alone, sideways if you’re getting there, down if you’re lost.”
  2. Show10 seconds“Eyes closed. Show me on three.”
  3. Scan10 secondsCount roughly: mostly up, mixed or mostly down. Note who is sideways or down.
  4. Decide10 secondsThumbs up keep working on the next problem. Sideways and down bring their work to one table for a two-minute reteach. If most thumbs are down, reteach everyone.

Why it works

Quiet students often hide confusion, and pacing by instinct misses it. A quick signal from everyone gives you a read on the room in seconds. One caution: thumbs show how confident students feel, not what they understand, so when you need proof, follow with Boards Up!.

Watch out for

  • Trusting thumbs alone: confident students can still be wrong. When it matters, follow with Boards Up! to see every answer.
  • Peer pressure: signals close to the chest, or eyes closed.
  • Not acting on it: decide your next move before you ask.

Level up

  • A thumbs-up student runs the help table.
  • Students who show a thumbs up explain the step to a sideways neighbor.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: gestures need no language.
  • IEP and 504 supports: use colored cards (green, yellow, red) on desks.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyGoogle Forms with branchingOne check question routes students automatically: confident goes to extension, unsure goes to a hint and more practice.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Write 6 quick check questions on [topic] for a mid-lesson pulse check, each answerable in 30 seconds. For each, say what I do next if most get it right, if it's mixed and if most get it wrong. Before you finish: The questions must show real understanding, not just recall. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
