Circle Up Check

One word. Everyone. Go.

The move

Every student shares one word or a 1–5 rating about how they’re arriving, in a quick round with no discussion.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 1Set the prompt10 seconds
  2. 2Think10 seconds
  3. 3Go around60 seconds
  1. Set the prompt10 seconds“One word for how you’re showing up today. Or a number, 1 to 5.”
  2. Think10 seconds“Got it? Thumbs up when ready.”
  3. Go around60 secondsStart with a volunteer and go in seat order. No comments, just “thank you.”
  4. Pass optionAlways“You can always say ‘pass.’”
  5. Note and follow upAfterQuietly check in later with anyone who gave a 1 or 2, or a worrying word.

See how it works

See it in action

“One word for how you feel about today’s test.”

Watch out for

  • Long stories: one word means one word.
  • Silly answers: laugh once and move on; it still builds community.
  • Sensitive answers: never respond in front of the class; follow up privately.

Level up

  • Tie the word to the content: “One word you remember from yesterday.”
  • Students pass the turn with a nod, no hands needed, so the round runs without you.

Why it works

A quick round tells you who might need a check-in before the lesson starts, and it gives every student a voice in the first minute. Hearing classmates also builds a sense that the room is a community. Keep it short and optional so it stays low-pressure.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: allow a word in any language, or a number.
  • IEP and 504 supports: let students hold up fingers or a feelings chart instead of speaking.

Tech option

A word-cloud poll shows the whole class’s mood at once. Talking is faster.
