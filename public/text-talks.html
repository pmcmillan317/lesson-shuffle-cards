The move

Partners read a chunk, pause, and each must say something before moving on: a prediction, a question or a connection.

Step by step

  4. 5Share out2 minutes
  1. Post the three options30 seconds“I predict…” / “Why did…?” or “How does…?” / “This connects to…”
  2. Read chunk 12 minutesPartners read silently or take turns aloud.
  3. Say something1 minute“Stop. Each partner says something: a prediction, a why or how question, or a connection to something we learned.”
  4. Repeat3–5 chunks
  5. Share out2 minutes“Which partner comment helped you most?”

Why it works

Silent reading can hide students who are just moving their eyes. Stopping to say something makes each reader think about what they just read, and hearing a partner’s thought adds a second way of seeing it. This routine is called “Say Something.”

Watch out for

  • “I don’t know”: require one of the three starters.
  • Pairs racing ahead: mark stopping points in the text.
  • Off-topic talk: keep each pause to one minute.

Level up

  • No repeats allowed, so each comment must be a different type.
  • Add a fourth option: “I disagree with the author because…”

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: starters posted in English and home languages.
  • IEP and 504 supports: the partner reads aloud; offer audio.
  • Early finishers: write their best comment as a margin note.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyGoogle Docs comments at stopping pointsPartners type their prediction, question or connection as comments and reply to each other. You can read every pair’s thinking afterward.
  • AugmentCommonLit (free)Free texts by grade with built-in annotation and questions.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Here is a text: [paste text]. Mark 4 stopping points. At each one, give a model prediction, a model "why/how" question and a model connection, so I can demonstrate Text Talks before partners try it. Before you finish: Stopping points should land where a prediction is genuinely possible. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
