The move

Show one anonymous wrong answer, your “favorite no,” and have the class find what’s right about it, what went wrong and how to fix it.

Step by step

Before class: Collect

  1. CollectBefore classSort exit tickets into right and wrong. Pick a wrong answer that many students made and that shows some good thinking.
  2. Show it30 secondsCopy it by hand so the handwriting isn’t recognizable.“This is my favorite no. It’s my favorite because there’s a lot right here.”
  3. What’s right?1–2 minutes“Turn and talk. What did this person do well?”
  4. What went wrong?1–2 minutes“Where does it go off track?”
  5. Fix it1–2 minutesStudents rewrite it correctly. One shares.

Why it works

Analyzing a real mistake shows students exactly where thinking goes wrong, which a polished model can’t. Starting with what’s right keeps it safe, and using a classmate’s (anonymous) work makes it feel relevant. This routine is often called “My Favorite No.”

Watch out for

  • Identifiable work: always rewrite it yourself.
  • Skipping “what’s right”: it keeps the room safe.
  • Random errors: pick the mistake many students made.

Level up

  • Students plant a mistake for classmates to catch.
  • Show two wrong answers and ask which is “more wrong,” and why.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: starters: “This part is right because…” / “The mistake is…”
  • IEP and 504 supports: give a printed copy to mark up.
  • Early finishers: explain how to avoid the mistake next time.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • AugmentGoogle LensCopy the handwriting on an exit ticket into typed text in seconds, so the favorite no is truly anonymous.
  • RedefineStudent-made error videosStudents record a short screencast explaining a common mistake and how to fix it, and build a class library of “favorite nos.”

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Write 3 student-style wrong answers to this problem: [problem]. Each should show a different common mistake but get a lot right. For each, list what is right, the exact step where it goes wrong, and the fix. Before you finish: Make the mistakes realistic, the kind students in [grade] actually make. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
