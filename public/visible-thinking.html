The move

Students look closely at an image or object and record what they see, what they think it means and what it makes them wonder.

  1. Show it30 secondsNo explanation yet.
  2. See2 minutes“Write only what you can see. No guesses yet. ‘I see…’”
  3. Think2 minutes“What do you think is going on? Point to what makes you say that.”
  4. Wonder1–2 minutes“What questions does it raise? ‘I wonder…’”

Why it works

Students often jump to an answer without looking closely. Separating observation (see) from interpretation (think) slows them down and makes them back up ideas with evidence. The “wonder” step shows you what they’re curious about and what they misunderstand.

Watch out for

  • Guesses in the “see” column: ask, “Can everyone see that, or is it a guess?”
  • “Think” with no evidence: always ask, “What makes you say that?”
  • Wonders that go nowhere: return to them later in the lesson or unit.

Level up

  • Wonders become tomorrow’s questions.
  • Reveal the image a section at a time and repeat.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: “see” can be single words or labels on a printed copy.
  • IEP and 504 supports: provide a three-column organizer.
  • Early finishers: find one detail no one else noticed.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • AugmentGoogle Arts & CultureZoomable high-resolution images let students see details no printout shows.
  • ModifyPadlet See / Think / Wonder columnsEvery student posts, and the Wonders stay up to answer later in the unit.
  • RedefineGoogle Earth TimelapseUse a place changing over decades as the image. Students observe change itself, not just a snapshot.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Suggest 5 images or objects for See/Think/Wonder in a lesson on [topic], each with what students will likely see, think and wonder, and how it leads into the lesson. Tell me where to find each image (public-domain or museum collections). Before you finish: Only suggest images you are confident exist; describe them so I can search. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
