Boards Up!

Everyone answers. Boards up!

The move

Every student writes an answer, then everyone shows it at the same moment so you can see the whole room’s thinking at once.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 2Ask a short question10 seconds
  2. 3Think time20–30 seconds
  3. 4Reveal5 seconds
  4. 5Scan and decide10 seconds

Before class: Hand out boards

  1. Hand out boardsOnce per classBoards, markers and an eraser or sock at each seat.
  2. Ask a short question10 secondsThe answer should be a number, a word, a short phrase or a quick sketch.“Write your answer. Keep your board face down until I say.”
  3. Think time20–30 secondsCount down out loud: “3, 2, 1…”
  4. Reveal5 secondsEveryone holds their board up at the same time.“Boards up!”
  5. Scan and decide10 secondsAbout 4 in 5 right: move on. A split: have neighbors compare boards and explain. Fewer than half right: reteach before asking again.
  6. Reset“Boards down, wipe clean.”

At a glance

Grouping
Whole class.

Any time you need to know whether everyone got it before moving on.

Why it works

When you call on raised hands, you hear from the students who already know. When everyone answers at once, you see the whole class in ten seconds and can decide whether to move on, regroup or reteach based on evidence instead of a hunch. Every student has to commit to an answer, but the stakes stay low.

Watch out for

  • Copying: boards stay face down until the signal.
  • Answers too long to scan: keep it to one word, number or sketch.
  • Doodling: caps on markers whenever boards are down.
  • Looking without acting: decide your next move before you ask, so you know what you’ll do with each result.

Level up

  • Ask a question with more than one right answer and have students compare.
  • Let a student write the next question while you hold a board too.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: accept sketches, numbers and single words.
  • IEP and 504 supports: offer A/B/C/D answer cards in place of writing, or a thicker marker.
  • Early finishers: write a second way to get the same answer on the back.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyWhiteboard.fiEvery student’s board is visible to you live and private to classmates. Project anonymous examples to discuss.
  • ModifyAmplify Classroom (Desmos) sketch screensStudents sketch or graph. Your dashboard shows every response, and you can hide names when you project.
  • AugmentPlickersFor rooms without devices: printed cards, one phone scan.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Write 10 quick whiteboard questions on [topic] whose answers fit on a board in under 30 seconds (a number, a word or a quick sketch). For each, name the most common wrong answer and what it tells me to reteach. Before you finish: Order them from easiest to hardest. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
