The move

Post today’s goal and two or three “I can” statements, and check them with students at the start, middle and end of class.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 2Start30 seconds
  2. 3Middle30 seconds
  3. 4End1 minute

Before class: Write it

  1. Write itBefore classGoal: “Today we’re learning to…” plus 2–3 “I can…” statements a student could check off.
  2. Start30 seconds“Here’s today’s mission. Read the ‘I can’ statements with me.”
  3. Middle30 seconds“Look at the frame. Which ‘I can’ are we working on now? Thumbs: how close are you?”
  4. End1 minute“Rate yourself 1, 2 or 3 on each one. 3 means you could teach it.”

Why it works

Students learn more when they know what they’re aiming for and what success looks like. Clear goals and success criteria let students judge their own progress, and checking them during the lesson turns a poster into a working tool.

Watch out for

  • Activities instead of goals (“I can complete the worksheet”): name the skill, not the task.
  • Posting and forgetting: the middle check is what makes it work.
  • Jargon: write it so a student could explain it.

Level up

  • Students rate themselves 1–3 on each before they leave.
  • Students write tomorrow’s “I can” based on what they still need.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: add an icon or example beside each statement.
  • IEP and 504 supports: give a printed copy to check off at the desk.
  • Early finishers: write a “challenge” statement for themselves.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • ModifyGoogle Forms self-ratingAt the end of class students rate 1–3 on each “I can.” Conditional formatting shows who needs what tomorrow.
  • AugmentClassroomscreen text widgetKeep the goal and “I can” statements on screen next to the timer all class.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Turn this objective into a student-friendly goal and 3 "I can" statements: [objective]. Each statement should be something a student could check off, with one quick way I could see the evidence. Before you finish: Use verbs students can observe themselves doing. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.

Build a classroom app

Build a single-file web app (HTML, CSS and JavaScript in one file) for my [grade] class. Make a lesson goal screen: I type today's goal and up to 3 "I can" statements; it shows them big all class, and at the end lets students rate 1-3 on each statement anonymously on one shared device, showing a summary chart. Requirements: no sign-in, no outside services, nothing saved except in this browser; large text that reads on a projector; works on a Chromebook; every control reachable by keyboard; clean, friendly design. Show me the working app, then tell me how to change the content.

Works in Gemini Canvas, ChatGPT Canvas or Claude. Each gives you a working app you can preview, tweak by asking for changes and share by link.
