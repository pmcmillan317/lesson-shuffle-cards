The move

Post today’s goal and two or three “I can” statements, and check them with students at the start, middle and end of class.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 2Start30 seconds
  2. 3Middle30 seconds
  3. 4End1 minute

Before class: Write it

  1. Write itBefore classGoal: “Today we’re learning to…” plus 2–3 “I can…” statements a student could check off.
  2. Start30 seconds“Here’s today’s mission. Read the ‘I can’ statements with me.”
  3. Middle30 seconds“Look at the frame. Which ‘I can’ are we working on now? Thumbs: how close are you?”
  4. End1 minute“Rate yourself 1, 2 or 3 on each one. 3 means you could teach it.”

See how it works

See it in action

“I can find the main idea. I can find two details that support it.”

“I can graph a line from an equation. I can find the slope from a graph.”

“I can identify an author’s claim. I can evaluate the strength of the evidence.”

Watch out for

  • Activities instead of goals (“I can complete the worksheet”): name the skill, not the task.
  • Posting and forgetting: the middle check is what makes it work.
  • Jargon: write it so a student could explain it.

Level up

  • Students rate themselves 1–3 on each before they leave.
  • Students write tomorrow’s “I can” based on what they still need.

Why it works

Students learn more when they know what they’re aiming for and what success looks like. Clear goals and success criteria let students judge their own progress, and checking them during the lesson turns a poster into a working tool.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: add an icon or example beside each statement.
  • IEP and 504 supports: give a printed copy to check off at the desk.
  • Early finishers: write a “challenge” statement for themselves.

Tech option

Put the frame on your first slide and return to it on your last.
