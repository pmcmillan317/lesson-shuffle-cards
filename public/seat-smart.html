The move

Match the room layout to the task: rows for solo work, clusters for collaboration, a circle for discussion.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 4Call the layout10 seconds
  2. 5Time it1 minute

Before class: Choose 2–3 layouts; Map them; Teach the move

  1. Choose 2–3 layoutsOnceRows (solo work, tests), clusters of 4 (group work), a circle or U (discussion).
  2. Map themOncePost a simple diagram of each with a name: “Rows,” “Pods,” “Circle.”
  3. Teach the move5 minutes, once“When I say ‘Pods,’ push desks into groups of four like this diagram. Lift, don’t drag.”Practice it.
  4. Call the layout10 seconds“Next is group work. Pods, go!”
  5. Time it1 minuteCount down. Celebrate improvements.

Why it works

Seating shapes how students interact. Reviews of the research find that rows tend to support focused independent work, while clusters support group interaction. Changing the layout to fit the task is a non-verbal cue for what kind of work comes next.

Watch out for

  • Noise and scraping: teach “lift, don’t drag.”
  • Fixed furniture: move students instead of desks, such as turning chairs to face a partner.
  • Changing too often: one switch per lesson is plenty.

Level up

  • Students rebuild the room in under a minute.
  • Students suggest the best layout for tomorrow’s task.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: seat them near a supportive peer in every layout.
  • IEP and 504 supports: keep preferential seating consistent across layouts.

Tech option

Put the layout diagram on a slide when you call the move.
