The move

Use the same cue for every transition and practice until students switch tasks in under a minute.

Step by step

Step timing

  1. 3Preview the next task10 seconds

Before class: Pick one cue; Teach it

  1. Pick one cueOnceExample: a chime, then a three-step call: “Finish. Stand. Shift.”
  2. Teach it3 minutes, once“When you hear the chime, finish your sentence, stand, and move to the next thing. Let’s practice.”
  3. Preview the next task10 seconds“Next, you’ll move to pairs and open to problem 2.”
  4. Cue and count10–30 secondsChime, then count down out loud or on a visible timer.
  5. Reset if needed30 secondsIf it takes too long, calmly practice once more.
  6. Name it5 seconds“22 seconds. New class record.”

Why it works

Messy transitions quietly eat instructional time. A consistent cue tells students exactly what’s happening without new directions each time, and practice turns it into a habit. Explicitly taught routines are a core part of effective classroom management.

Watch out for

  • Different cues every time: one cue for all transitions.
  • Directions after the cue: preview the next task first, then cue.
  • Giving up on it: expect a week of practice before it’s smooth.

Level up

  • Post the class record and try to break it.
  • A student runs the cue.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: pair the sound cue with a hand signal.
  • IEP and 504 supports: give a 1-minute private warning before the class cue.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Design a transition routine for [grade]: one consistent cue, a 3-word call, a 3-minute teaching script with a practice round, and a way to track and celebrate faster times. Before you finish: The routine must work for every kind of transition, not one. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.

Build a classroom app

Build a single-file web app (HTML, CSS and JavaScript in one file) for my [grade] class. Make a transition timer: one big button plays a chime and starts a count-up timer; another stops it and records the time; it shows today's best and the all-time record. Requirements: no sign-in, no outside services, nothing saved except in this browser; large text that reads on a projector; works on a Chromebook; every control reachable by keyboard; clean, friendly design. Show me the working app, then tell me how to change the content.

Works in Gemini Canvas, ChatGPT Canvas or Claude. Each gives you a working app you can preview, tweak by asking for changes and share by link.
