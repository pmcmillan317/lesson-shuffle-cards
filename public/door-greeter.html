The move

Stand at the door and greet each student by name with a quick, positive word before they walk in.

Step by step

  1. Post a warm-upBefore passing timeA short task on the board that students start as soon as they sit down.
  2. Stand at the doorOne foot in the hall, one in the room, so you can see both.
  3. Greet by nameAbout 5 seconds each“Morning, Jordan! Good to see you.”Add a handshake, high five, fist bump or wave.
  4. Notice and nudgeIf a student seems off: “Rough morning? Glad you’re here.” If someone needs a reminder: “Warm-up’s on the board. Get started.”
  5. Close the door and begin on time

Why it works

A personal greeting tells each student they belong here, and it gives you a quick read on everyone’s mood before class starts. In a study of middle school classrooms, teachers who greeted students positively at the door saw more time spent engaged in learning and less disruptive behavior. It costs no class time.

Watch out for

  • Hallway duty at the same time: greet from the doorway so you can see the hall too.
  • Conversations that run long: keep it to a few seconds and continue later.
  • Skipping students who are hard to reach: they often need the greeting most.
  • Late arrivals: greet them quietly at their seat so the routine stays warm.

Level up

  • Ask each student one quick question from yesterday as they walk in. A two-second answer at the door is daily review before class even starts.
  • Let students choose their own greeting.
  • Learn one personal detail about each student and use it in your greeting within two weeks.

Make it work for everyone

  • Multilingual learners: learn to say hello in students’ home languages.
  • Students with sensory needs: always offer a no-touch option such as a wave or a nod.
  • Students with a tough history at school: a consistent, warm greeting every day matters more than what you say.

Tech upgrades

Free tools that change the task, not just the medium.

  • AugmentClassroomscreen or a Google Slides do-nowHave the warm-up on screen before students arrive so you can stay at the door.
  • ModifyGoogle Forms do-nowStudents answer the warm-up in a form, so you can read every response before the lesson starts.

AI prompts

Copy, fill in the [brackets] and paste into Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude. Leave out student names and personal details.

Make the materials

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are working on [topic]. Give me 10 do-now warm-ups for [topic] that students can start without any directions from me, each under 3 minutes, mixing recall, a quick write and a puzzle. Before you finish: Each must be doable with only paper and pencil. Write at a level my students can read. If anything above is unclear, ask me up to 3 questions before you start.
